\subsection{Milestone 1: Inception}

\subsubsection{Selection of problem and requirements engineering for ML}

\paragraph{Problem selection.} Modern text-to-image generators (Stable Diffusion, DALL-E, MidJourney, \dots) produce photorealistic images at scale, fuelling misinformation, fake reviews, and trust problems for platforms and media. Reliably telling a \textbf{real photograph} from an \textbf{AI-generated image} cannot be solved with hand-written rules, the cues are subtle, generator-specific, and evolving, which makes it a genuine machine-learning problem. We target real-vs-AI detection in particular because nowadays it is increasingly difficult to recognise whether a piece of media is real or AI-generated.

\paragraph{ML goal.} Build, deploy, and operate an ML component that, given an input image, decides whether it is real or AI-generated, exposed as a monitored production service. Our main task is binary image classification (real vs \texttt{ai\_generated}), and optionally we might as well classify AI-generated images among the 5 generators (which generator model owns the image). Our focus is mostly on engineering around the model (reproducibility, QA, deployment, monitoring), not maximizing raw accuracy.

\paragraph{What makes it unique.} Unlike general image-classification tasks whose target classes are usually obvious to a human (weather, animals, traffic signs, \dots), we target a category that in the near future may be very hard to classify even for people --- telling a real image from an AI-generated one. Our component also covers \textbf{five recent} generators (2022--2024), and instead of being specific to one dataset, our component would be easily configured and used with different, bigger datasets.

\paragraph{Intended use.} As main use case of our ML component we can mention reliable platforms (such as news, social media, \dots) to distinguish real from fake (or generated) images to prevent misinformation and correctly flag likely AI-generated uploads via an API; another use case could be to quickly check the authenticity of an image with a confidence score, which is a well-documented and reproducible component for downstream developers to integrate in a larger system.

\paragraph{ML component specification (I/O contract).}
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]

\medskip

  \item \textbf{Input:} one image (JPEG/PNG/WEBP), bounded file size; preprocessed identically to training (fixed square resize + uniform re-encode).

\medskip

  \item \textbf{Output:} JSON \texttt{\{"label": "real" | "ai\_generated", "score": <float 0--1>\}}; optionally generator model as well.

\medskip

  \item \textbf{Operating context:} stateless REST service, containerised, with health and metrics endpoints.
\end{itemize}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Challenges.}
The project runs on general-purpose laptops and free-tier cloud, so we favour transfer learning on a small model, and the dataset must fit a free-tier DVC remote ($\sim$15\,GB) --- which Defactify ($\sim$7.5\,GB) does. We use the data under \textbf{CC BY 4.0} (non-commercial academic use, MS COCO), assuming inputs resemble the training distribution (everyday photos vs full AI images) rather than face-swaps or adversarial inputs. The main risks are:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]

\medskip

  \item \textbf{shortcut learning} --- the model keying on non-semantic cues such as image shape or format instead of ``AI-ness'' (mitigated by normalising size and re-encoding uniformly);

\medskip

  \item \textbf{class imbalance} of roughly 5:1 fake:real (class weights / generator-stratified sampling and balanced metrics);

\medskip


  \item \textbf{split leakage} from captions shared across train/val/test, which we already observed in EDA (a caption-grouped re-split);

\medskip

  \item \textbf{generator drift}, i.e.\ degraded accuracy on unseen or newer generators (leave-one-generator-out evaluation and drift monitoring).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Success criteria.} The component is successful when it is served via a  reproducible, containerised API, and meets the model targets on a leakage-free test set with shortcuts controlled, while all processes monitored for drift.

\subsubsection{Dataset card}
\textit{Summary of the dataset card; the full card lives in the repository (e.g., \texttt{docs/dataset\_card.md}). Source: \href{https://huggingface.co/datasets/Rajarshi-Roy-research/Defactify_Image_Dataset}{Defactify / MS-COCOAI on Hugging Face} (\texttt{Rajarshi-Roy-research/Defactify\_Image\_Dataset}), arXiv 2601.00553.}

\paragraph{Dataset summary.} Defactify (MS-COCOAI) pairs real photographs from \textbf{MS COCO} with AI-generated images produced from the \emph{same captions} by five recent text-to-image generators, for the task of detecting real vs.\ AI-generated images (and, optionally, attributing the generator).
 96{,}000 image--caption records: \textbf{16{,}000 real} (MS COCO) and \textbf{80{,}000 AI-generated} (16{,}000 each from Stable Diffusion 2.1, SDXL, Stable Diffusion 3, DALL-E 3, MidJourney v6).

\paragraph{Data fields.} Each record (row) of the dataset contains the following fields:

\smallskip
\noindent\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l Y l@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Field} & \textbf{Description} & \textbf{Data type} \\
\midrule
\texttt{Caption} & The text prompt used to generate the image, or the descriptive caption for real images & \texttt{string} \\
\texttt{Image} & The actual image content (e.g., \texttt{.jpg}, \texttt{.png}) & \texttt{Image} \\
\texttt{Label\_A} & \textbf{Task A:} binary label for image veracity & \texttt{ClassLabel} (int) \\
\texttt{Label\_B} & \textbf{Task B:} label specifying the generation source/model & \texttt{ClassLabel} (int) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\paragraph{How it was created.} The authors generated one synthetic image per MS COCO caption for each generator, so every real image has generated counterparts from the same prompt. This \textbf{controls for content bias}: a detector must key on \emph{how} an image was made, not \emph{what} it shows.

\paragraph{Intended uses.} Training and evaluating real-vs-AI classifiers (Task~A) and generator-attribution models (Task~B); studying cross-generator generalisation and drift.

\paragraph{Considerations, biases \& limitations} (from our exploratory analysis, data-exploration.ipynb):
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]

\medskip

  \item \textbf{Class imbalance:} the binary task is $\sim$5:1 fake:real (Task~B is balanced).

\medskip

  \item \textbf{Aspect-ratio shortcut:} generated images are almost all square while real photos are not ($\sim$100\% vs $\sim$4\% square in our sample) --- resize/normalise to avoid the model cheating on shape.

\medskip

  \item \textbf{Split leakage:} some captions appear across train/val/test; use a caption-grouped re-split before evaluation.

\medskip

  \item \textbf{Scope:} everyday COCO scenes and five specific 2022--2024 generators; not representative of deepfake face-swaps, adversarial inputs, or newer generators.

\medskip

  \item \textbf{Privacy/representation:} MS COCO photos may contain people and reflect MS COCO's own collection biases.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Licence \& citation.} \textbf{CC BY 4.0} (author-declared in arXiv 2601.00553); real images additionally follow MS COCO's terms. Attribute both the paper and MS COCO.

\subsubsection{Model card}
\textit{Model card following Mitchell et al.\ (2019). Written at inception and updated in Milestone~2 with the selected baseline (see \emph{Experiment tracking}); test-set results are completed in Milestone~3. The full card lives in the repository (\texttt{docs/model\_card.md}).}

\paragraph{Model details.} Real-vs-AI image classifier by \textbf{Team Vera} (UPC MLOps, 2026--27). Version 0.1 (Milestone~2 baseline, September 2026). Type: binary image classifier built by \textbf{transfer learning} --- the \textbf{frozen} image encoder of \textbf{CLIP ViT-B/32} (512-d embeddings) with a standardisation + \textbf{logistic-regression} head (C~$=0.01$, balanced class weights); the image is flagged as AI when $P(\text{AI}) \geq 0.57$, a threshold tuned on validation and stored inside the model. Selected among six baselines and a regularisation sweep tracked with MLflow. Trained on the Defactify dataset. Licence: code under the repository licence; data under CC BY 4.0. Contact: Team Vera (see cover).

\paragraph{Intended use.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]

\medskip

  \item \textbf{Primary uses:} decide whether an input image is real or AI-generated, with a confidence score (optionally attribute the generator).

\medskip

  \item \textbf{Primary users:} content-moderation/platform back-ends, journalists/fact-checkers, and developers integrating the component via its API.

\medskip

  \item \textbf{Out-of-scope:} deepfake face-swap or partial-manipulation forensics, adversarially-perturbed inputs, generators far outside the training set, and any high-stakes decision without human review.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Factors.} Relevant factors: generator family (SD2.1, SDXL, SD3, DALL-E 3, MJ v6), image resolution/aspect ratio, and content domain (COCO-like scenes). Evaluation is reported \emph{per generator} (leave-one-generator-out) as well as overall.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Balanced accuracy, macro-F1, PR-AUC, and recall on the minority (real) class; the decision threshold is tuned on validation rather than fixed at 0.5. Performance variation is reported across generators. (Targets: see MR-1--MR-4.)

\paragraph{Evaluation data.} The Defactify \textbf{test} split, re-split by caption to be leakage-free. Preprocessing: centre-crop/resize to a fixed square and uniform re-encoding so shape and format cannot leak the label.

\paragraph{Training data.} A caption-grouped subsample of Defactify (5{,}352 training images from 560 captions, same preprocessing). The $\sim$5:1 imbalance is handled by balanced class weights, which keep the head independent of the training class ratio, and by the tuned threshold.

\paragraph{Quantitative analyses.} On validation: balanced accuracy 0.935, macro-F1 0.884, recall (real) 0.947, PR-AUC (real) 0.933; recall per generator from 0.85 (SD~3) to 0.98 (DALL-E~3). Test-set results and the leave-one-generator-out drift experiment follow in Milestone~3.

\paragraph{Ethical considerations.} Dual-use (a detector can be evaded); false positives may wrongly flag genuine images, so outputs are advisory and expect human oversight. The dataset's scope and biases (above) bound where the model is valid. Training energy/CO$_2$ is tracked with CodeCarbon.

\paragraph{Caveats \& recommendations.} Guard against the aspect-ratio/format shortcut; expect accuracy to drop on unseen or newer generators (monitor drift and retrain as needed); the model is trained on modest-resolution, COCO-domain images and should not be assumed to transfer out-of-domain.

\subsubsection{Code versioning}
\label{sec:code-versioning}

The code is versioned with Git in a public repository of the course organisation on GitHub, following \textbf{GitHub Flow}: \texttt{main} is always deployable, and every change is developed in its own branch and merged through a reviewed pull request. The main rules, documented in \texttt{CONTRIBUTING.md}, are:

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Branches:} one branch per Jira story, named \texttt{<type>/<Jira key>-<description>}, e.g.\ \texttt{feat/KAN-12-mlflow-tracking}.
  \item \textbf{Commits:} small, related changes, with messages following \emph{Conventional Commits}~1.0.0 (\texttt{type(scope): description}), a body that explains what changed and why, and a \texttt{Refs: KAN-n} footer.
  \item \textbf{Pull requests:} titled like a commit or with the Jira key and the story summary, and filled in from a template (why, what is changing, how to test, and a checklist covering tests, credentials, data and documentation). A pull request needs the approval of another member before it can be merged.
  \item \textbf{History:} published history is never rewritten (no force pushes); conflicts are solved by merging \texttt{main} into the branch.
\end{itemize}

To minimise mistakes, GitHub enforces these rules instead of relying on discipline. \texttt{main} is protected: it only accepts changes through a pull request with one approval, also from administrators, and cannot be force-pushed or deleted. Pull requests are merged with a merge commit, so each member's commits stay in \texttt{main}, and merged branches are deleted automatically. Secret scanning with push protection blocks pushes that contain recognised credential formats; credentials are only kept in git-ignored local files (\texttt{.dvc/config.local}, \texttt{.env}), and \texttt{.gitignore} also excludes data, caches and local environments.

\subsubsection{Experiment tracking}

\paragraph{Tooling.} Training runs are tracked with \textbf{MLflow}, using the tracking server that \textbf{DagsHub} hosts for our repository (\url{https://dagshub.com/AdriSegurao/MLOps-Vera.mlflow}). The same DagsHub project already serves as our DVC remote, so code, data and experiments are browsable in one place, for free and shared by the whole team. Credentials live in a git-ignored \texttt{.env} file (template in \texttt{.env.example}); without it, runs fall back to a local SQLite store (\texttt{./mlflow.db}), so the pipeline and its tests never depend on the network.

\paragraph{Integration with DVC.} Model building is split into two DVC stages. \texttt{embed} passes every preprocessed image through a \emph{frozen} pretrained backbone and stores one embedding per image and split. \texttt{train} fits a standardisation + logistic-regression head on those embeddings, tunes the decision threshold on validation (maximising balanced accuracy, as the model card specifies) and logs the run to MLflow. Keeping the costly forward pass in its own stage means that changing only head parameters re-runs \texttt{train} alone, in seconds. An experiment is a variation of \texttt{params.yaml} launched with \texttt{dvc exp run -S <param>=<value>}: DVC records the exact code, parameters, data hashes and metrics of each experiment as a Git reference (\texttt{dvc exp show}, \texttt{dvc exp push}), while MLflow provides the visual comparison and stores the artefacts. The test split is never read by \texttt{train}; it is reserved for the final evaluation, so that choosing between experiments cannot overfit it.

\paragraph{What each run records.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Parameters:} the \texttt{embed} (backbone, batch size) and \texttt{train} (C, class weighting, iterations, seed) sections of \texttt{params.yaml}.
  \item \textbf{Metrics} on train and validation: the model-card metrics (balanced accuracy, macro-F1, PR-AUC and recall of the minority real class), plus ROC-AUC, the recall of each generator and the tuned threshold.
  \item \textbf{Traceability tags:} the pinned dataset revision, the DVC hash of the embeddings the head was trained on, and the backbone weights; MLflow adds the Git commit. Any run can therefore be tied to the exact code and data that produced it.
  \item \textbf{Artefacts:} the validation confusion matrix and precision--recall curve, and the fitted model with its input signature. The tuned threshold is baked into the logged model (scikit-learn's \texttt{FixedThresholdClassifier}), so its \texttt{predict()} gives the same decisions as the DVC-versioned model.
\end{itemize}
Runs are named \texttt{<backbone>-logreg-<balanced|unweighted>-C<C>} so they can be told apart at a glance in the MLflow UI.

\paragraph{Findings and model selection.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{The backbone dominates.} CLIP improves balanced accuracy by about 10 points over both ResNets and raises the PR-AUC of the real class from 0.56--0.66 to 0.90. It is the only backbone that meets MR-1 (balanced accuracy $\geq 0.85$). This is consistent with prior work showing that frozen CLIP features, learnt from web-scale image--text pairs, transfer well to generated-image detection (Ojha et al., CVPR 2023).
  \item \textbf{A bigger ImageNet backbone barely helps.} ResNet-50 gains one point over ResNet-18 but overfits more (0.96 train vs.\ 0.82 validation balanced accuracy); CLIP shows the smallest gap (0.96 vs.\ 0.92).
  \item \textbf{Class weighting hardly changes the scores.} Tuning the threshold on validation already compensates for the imbalance, so weighted and unweighted heads differ by less than one point, which is within noise for 209 real validation images.
  \item \textbf{SD~3 is the hardest generator} for every backbone (0.79 recall with CLIP), while SDXL and DALL-E~3 are the easiest; per-generator results will be studied further with the leave-one-generator-out experiment (MR-4).
\end{itemize}
We keep \textbf{CLIP ViT-B/32} as backbone and \textbf{balanced class weights}: although the scores barely differ here, weighting makes the head independent of the class ratio, so it can be retrained on data with a different imbalance without retuning.

\paragraph{Regularisation sweep.} With C~$=1$ the CLIP head fits the training set almost perfectly (train ROC-AUC 1.000 vs.\ 0.970 on validation). Since the embeddings are cached, a sweep of C only re-runs \texttt{train} (a few seconds per run); Table~\ref{tab:c-sweep} shows that stronger regularisation narrows the gap and improves every validation metric, with C~$=0.01$ best. It also brings the tuned threshold down from 0.99 to 0.57, i.e.\ the balanced head's probabilities are better calibrated.
